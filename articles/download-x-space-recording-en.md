# How to Download an X Space Recording (Free, No Login)

X Spaces are live audio conversations on X (formerly Twitter). A host opens a room, people join to talk or just listen, and when it's over, it's gone — unless someone saved it. X does keep a replay for a while, but replays expire, and there's no built-in download button. If you heard something worth keeping — an interview, a market discussion, a debate, a friend's late-night talk — you need to save it yourself, while it's still there.

That's what spkeep.com does. It's a free web tool that downloads X Spaces audio as an .m4a file. No account, no login, no app to install. You paste a link, check the details, and download.

But it's worth knowing that spkeep.com isn't only a downloader. It's also an archive — a library of X Space recordings you can browse, listen to, and download, even if you don't have a link of your own.

## Why save an X Space recording?

A few reasons people actually do it:

- **Replays disappear.** X doesn't keep them forever. A Space you meant to listen to later can be gone by the time you get back to it.
- **You can't listen live.** Spaces often run at awkward hours. Saving the audio means you can listen on your own time — on a commute, at the gym, offline.
- **You want to keep a record.** Journalists, researchers, and traders save Spaces as source material. A quote you'll need later is a lot easier to find in a file on your phone than in a replay link that may not load.
- **Audio is easier than a screen recording.** Recording a two-hour Space with your phone's screen recorder eats storage and battery, and the quality is worse. A direct audio download is smaller and cleaner.
- **You want to go back to it.** A good Space is often worth a second listen. Once it's downloaded, it's yours — no link rot, no expiry, no wondering whether X still has it.

## How to download an X Space recording with spkeep.com

It takes about a minute:

1. **Copy the Space link.** On X, open the Space (or a tweet that links to it) and copy the URL.
2. **Paste it at [spkeep.com](https://spkeep.com/).** The site reads the Space in guest mode, so you don't need to log in to X at all.
3. **Check the Space info.** Before you download, you can see the title, the host, and the total duration, so you know you're getting the right recording.
4. **Download.** One click, and you get the full audio as an .m4a file. It plays on any phone or computer — no special player needed.

That's it. There's nothing to install and nothing to sign up for. It works in the browser on your phone, which is where most people listen to Spaces anyway. Downloading an X Space on iPhone or Android works exactly the same way — open the site, paste the link, download the file.

## Live Spaces and trimming

spkeep.com works for ended Spaces (replays) and for live ones. If a Space is still going, you can start a recording and it keeps going until the Space ends, so you don't have to sit there watching it finish.

If you only want part of a replay — say, the twenty minutes where the actual discussion happened, not the hour of waiting-room chat before it — you can trim it down to just the part you want instead of downloading the whole thing.

## X Space transcript and AI summary

Besides the audio, spkeep.com can also turn a Space into a transcript and an AI summary, in the Space's own language. That's useful when you don't have two hours to listen but want to know what was said, or when you want to search the text for a specific topic or quote. The transcript is the full spoken text; the summary gives you the key points without the filler.

This matters more than it sounds. A two-hour Space is a big commitment. A summary lets you decide in two minutes whether the full recording is worth your evening. And if you're researching a topic across several Spaces, a searchable X Space transcript beats scrubbing through audio every time.
